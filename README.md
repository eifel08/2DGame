# 2DGame — Metroidvania Core (Godot 4)

The core engine for an original 2D Action-Metroidvania.
It is a native Godot 4 project written in GDScript.
It has no plugins and no add-ons.

Tested with **Godot 4.3**. Newer 4.x versions open it too; Godot may offer to upgrade the project files.

## Clone and run

1. Install Godot 4.3 or newer (the standard build, not .NET): https://godotengine.org/download
2. Clone the repository:
   ```bash
   git clone https://github.com/eifel08/2DGame.git
   ```
   Or on GitHub: **Code → Download ZIP**, then unzip.
3. Start Godot. In the Project Manager click **Import**, select `2DGame/project.godot`, then **Import & Edit**.
4. The first opening imports the assets. This takes a few seconds.
5. Press **F5** (or ▶) to play. The title screen opens: choose **Play**, then a save slot.

To pull later changes: `git pull` inside the `2DGame` folder, then reopen the project.

Keys can be rebound in **Settings → Controls** (title screen or pause menu). The defaults live in **Project → Project Settings → Input Map** (generated by `tools/build_assets.gd`).

- **One key and one gamepad button per action.** The list shows both (e.g. `H · L3`). There are no hidden extra keys: WASD and the old alternates (Z, X, C, V, B, N, F, P, Shift) do nothing unless you bind them.
- **No duplicates.** Binding a key or button that another action holds gives that action your old key (a swap), so each key triggers one action only. A conflict in an old settings file leaves the other action unbound (shown as `-`).
- **Rebinding:** confirm a button, then press a key or a gamepad button. Esc cancels. The debug keys (F1, `, F2, R) cannot be taken.
Every menu (title, slot select, settings, pause, inventory) works with the mouse and with the keyboard or gamepad.
- Arrows (or D-pad / stick) move the focus. Enter or Space confirms. A click works too.
- Only one button is highlighted at a time (gold frame). Hovering with the mouse moves that highlight.
- Up and Down wrap around, so the first and last buttons are always reachable. In Settings, Down goes all the way to **Back**.

## Systems

### Movement
> **Found, not given:** a new game can run, jump, attack and heal. Double jump, dash, wall climb (wall slide + wall jumps) and the grappling hook are **items** found in the world (see *Movement items* below).

Crisp acceleration and stopping, dynamic jump height, double jump, dash with i-frames, coyote time and jump buffering. Gravity defaults to 2100, a little low, to give more reaction time for pogo chains.

**Walls:** press into a wall while falling and **wall friction** slows you to a slide. Press jump to wall-jump. Wall jumps have a fixed height (a quick tap is enough). Holding toward the wall as you jump makes a "climb jump" with a smaller push, so you swing back and can **climb a single wall** with consecutive wall jumps. A wall jump or slide refreshes your double jump and air dash.

### Combat
Directional attacks (left, right, up, down) and the downward pogo bounce off enemies and spikes.
The pogo is forgiving: the downward swing has a wide hitbox (78 × 80 px, `Config.combat.pogo_hitbox`), stays active a little longer, and an attack press is buffered for 0.16 s. Hits trigger hitstop and screen shake. Enemy contact deals damage, knocks you back and starts the i-frames.

### Grappling hook
Press **H** (or **B**) to throw the hook. It flies out up to **256 px** (8 tiles, `Config.grapple.max_range`).

**Aim and mode** (read when you throw). Exactly three cases; the direction always follows the side you **face**:

| Key | State | Angle | Action |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Up | Grounded or airborne | 90° (straight up) | Pull |
| Down | Airborne only | 45° up, facing side | Pendulum swing |
| Down | Grounded | 0° (straight ahead) | Pull |
| Left / Right / none | Grounded or airborne | 0° (straight ahead) | Pull |

- **Left / Right on the throw frame win:** if you hold Left or Right as you throw, you turn that way on that very frame, before the trajectory is computed. The hook always goes the held way, even during a wall slide (which faces you away from the wall).
- "Airborne" means not on the floor (`is_on_floor()`). Input and airborne are read on the frame you press the hook key, even when the throw itself is buffered.

- **Forgiving:** the hook catches a surface or an enemy up to **12 px** beside its path (`hook_radius`). It sends three parallel rays for terrain, and a box that wide for enemies. Hazards are checked with a thin tip (`hazard_radius`), so the tolerance never makes spikes easier to hit.
- **Hold while it flies:** the moment you throw, you hang still in the air until the hook latches or misses, so you do not drop and snag on a wall first. On the ground you simply stand.

**Pull mode**
- **Walls and ceilings:** the hook holds and pulls you there. The pull eases in, then runs at `pull_speed`, without gravity.
- **One-way platforms:** the hook latches on them too. The pull carries you up through the platform and sets you down on top of it.
- **Enemies:** touching an enemy does **not** hurt it. You are pulled toward it and stop **18 px** short of its contact hitbox (`enemy_safe_margin`). There you **rebound**, nearly a full pogo high (`enemy_bounce_mul` × the pogo speed), and a little away. **Only then** does the enemy take your standard attack damage (`Config.combat.attack_damage`, with Ascendant Form's bonus). A pull you cancel on the way deals no damage. The hook does not fill the energy meter; only basic attacks do.
- **Ending a pull:** you arrive, something blocks you, or you cancel: **Jump** (a fresh jump), **Dash**, or the hook key again.

**Swing mode** (Down + hook in the air)
- On a wall, ceiling or platform the hook does **not** pull. It becomes a rope of fixed length, and you swing like a pendulum. Gravity alone drives the arc; there is no air resistance, so you climb as high on the far side as you started.
- An enemy hit in swing mode is handled like a pull (enemies are never swing anchors).
- The swing lets go **at once** when:
  1. you press **Jump** (you jump off, keeping the swing's speed), the **hook key** or **Dash**;
  2. you take damage, from any source;
  3. you touch a wall, floor or ceiling;
  4. the rope crosses an enemy or a hazard.
- Letting go keeps the swing's momentum, so you can fling yourself across a gap.

**For both modes**
- **Hazards:** the hook never latches on spikes, on floors, walls or ceilings. If its path touches a hazard it is deflected (a small spark) and comes back.
- **Double jump:** once the hook has latched, the end of the pull or swing gives your double jump back, however it ends. A jump off the hook is a free jump, so the double jump stays.
- **No protection:** you are not invulnerable on the hook.
- **Ignored:** the hidden-wall layers. An **undiscovered** hidden pocket wall stops the hook like rock, so the hook never reaches into a covered pocket.
- **Misses** fly back and end the hold. After the hook is back there is a short cooldown (0.3 s).
- Tuning: `Config.grapple` and the debug overlay (*Grapple Hook*: range, speeds, tolerance, safe gap, bounce, cooldown). The debug toggle *Grapple Hook* turns the ability off.

**Grapple pegs** (`world/objects/grapple_peg.tscn`, letter `+` in a room layout)
- A peg is an anchor point only the hook touches. It is an `Area2D` alone on physics layer 7 `grapple_peg`. No body collides with that layer, so you and enemies pass straight through it.
- **Swing throw** (Down in the air): you swing around the peg, exactly like a swing on a ceiling.
- **Any other throw:** the hook pulls you to the peg and **stays attached**. You hang still under it (no gravity, zero velocity).
- **During the hold:** Left / Right only turn you, so you can aim your next move. Any other action lets go at once and still does its job: Jump jumps (with the double jump back), Dash dashes, Attack and Spell fire, Up / Down just let go, the hook key only lets go. Pause, Inventory and the debug keys leave the hold alone. Taking damage also lets go.
- **Three kinds**, one scene each: standard `grapple_peg.tscn` (`+`, gold), moving `grapple_peg_moving.tscn` (`m`, cyan with a ring and a dashed path line), breakable `grapple_peg_breakable.tscn` (`b`, red with cracks). A moving peg carries its rope: a hold rides along under it, a swing follows its anchor. A breakable peg starts to shake when the hook latches; after `crumble_delay` (1 s) it crumbles, the hook lets go and you drop. It is back after `respawn_time` (3 s).
- Test room: two pegs over the right spike pit. Stand on the block left of the pit and throw right: you hang from the low peg. The high one is 45° up-right of it: press Down to let go, then Down + hook in the air to swing on it.

### Energy, healing and spells
- **One shared energy meter.** Basic attacks fill it.
- **Two ways to heal:**
  1. **Channel (risk and reward):** hold L on the ground. Healing comes in **chunks**.
     - While a chunk channels, energy drains and the health it will give appears as a **white** segment on the health bar.
     - When the chunk completes, the white segment turns **red**: that health is now yours. A short pause follows, so you can let go safely. Keep holding to start the next chunk.
     - If you let go early, jump or get hit before the chunk completes, the drained energy is **lost** and no health returns.
     - You cannot start without enough energy for a whole chunk.
     - Chunk size, energy cost, duration and pause are in `Config.vitals` (`heal_chunk_*`) and in the debug overlay.
  2. **Save at a checkpoint:** see below. Saving restores full health.
- **Heal-ready glow:** the energy bar glows green, with a light sweep, whenever it holds enough energy for one full heal chunk. A small green dot under the bar marks a chunk's cost.
- **One Spell button (U / N, gamepad Y) plus a direction.** The direction held when you press it picks the spell:

  | Input | Spell |
  | --- | --- |
  | Spell alone | **Mana Discharge**, always. It is fixed to this input for good: it cannot be unequipped, moved or forgotten. |
  | Spell + Up | The **Up** slot |
  | Spell + Left / Right | The **Side** slot, cast toward that side (you turn that way). One slot serves both sides. |
  | Spell + Down | The **Down** slot |

  - Up wins over Down, and both win over Left / Right. An empty slot casts nothing; it never falls back to Mana Discharge. A **locked** slot casts nothing too.
  - A new game knows **no** equippable spell, and the Up, Side and Down slots are **locked** (see *Progression* below). Mana Discharge is the only spell from the start.
  - Saves store the three directional slots and which of them are open. A save from before progression loads with every slot open. A save from the old numbered hotkeys loads with the default loadout (`Game.DEFAULT_LOADOUT`: Up **Radiant Lance**, Side **Comet Dash**, Down **Meteor Slam**).
- **Mana Discharge** (the neutral spell, `ultimates/abilities/mana_discharge.gd`):
  - It **always drains ALL your energy**.
  - With at least **20** energy (`cost`), it releases a wave. The wave's full radius scales with your **maximum** energy: `radius_per_energy` × max energy, 80 px at the starting 50, 160 px at 100. Mana Potions widen it.
  - The wave deals **minimal damage** (4) to every enemy it passes, once each.
  - With less than 20 energy it still casts and still drains what is left, but it **fails**: a small broken ring flickers and is gone almost at once. No damage.
- **The eight equippable spells.** Energy thresholds are defaults in `Config.ultimates` and can be tuned live in the debug overlay.

  | Spell | Type | Energy | What it does |
  | --- | --- | --- | --- |
  | Nova Burst | AoE blast | 33 | Expanding shockwave around the player. |
  | Spirit Volley | Projectiles | 50 | Fan of homing bolts. |
  | Ascendant Form | Buff | 100 | More damage, speed and reach for a while. |
  | Comet Dash | Mobility strike | 25 | Invulnerable horizontal charge toward facing (Up / Down ignored), then a blast. |
  | **Repulse Aegis** | Counter (parry) | 20 minimum, **consumes all energy** | No wind-up. Opens a **0.2 s** counter window. If an enemy touches you inside it (or comes within 6 px of your body), its contact damage is negated and it takes **150 %** of the energy consumed. **Enemy contact only:** projectiles are not blocked and hurt as usual. Cast too early and the energy is wasted. |
  | **Stasis Field** | Emergency escape | 80 | Near-instant (0.05 s) pulse: enemies and hostile projectiles within **90 px** freeze in place for **0.8 s**. A last-second escape, not area control. Frozen enemies cannot move or hurt you but can still be hit. Frozen projectiles hang harmless, then fly on. |
  | **Radiant Lance** | Beam | 60 | A long piercing horizontal beam toward facing (Up / Down ignored). It stops at walls and hits each enemy once. |
  | **Meteor Slam** | Dive strike | 30 | Invulnerable straight-down plunge that hits enemies on the way, then a wide ground shockwave and short landing i-frames. Cast in the air, it dives at once. **Cast on the ground, you first hop up** (about 0.25 s): the hop is a **vulnerable** wind-up, and a hit during it cancels the slam. |
- **Color-coded energy bar, no numbers.** Mana Discharge and each equipped slot put a mark on the bar at the energy they need. A mark is bright when there is enough energy.
- **Spell cross (HUD, right of the bars).** A D-pad diagram, `Hud/SpellCross` with the child nodes `Up`, `Left`, `Center`, `Right`, `Down` (`ui/spell_cross.gd`, `ui/spell_node_view.gd`). Mana Discharge sits in the center. The Up and Down slots are above and below it. The Side slot appears on **both** the left and the right; the two always match. Each node is framed in its energy-tier color and is bright when affordable. The Spell key (U by default, follows rebinding) shows in the cross's top-left corner.
  - The color follows the energy tier, not the spell: green (low), yellow, orange, pink (full).
  - Empty slots have no mark.
- **No exact stats for the player.** Abilities, tooltips and lore never show energy numbers. Each ability shows a small gauge filled to its requirement instead. Exact values appear only in the debug stats view.

### Progression: low start, potions, scrolls and meditation
A new game starts weak. Everything else is found in the world.

| At the start | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Max health | **50** (`HealthEnergy.STARTING_MAX_HEALTH`) |
| Max energy | **50** (`HealthEnergy.STARTING_MAX_ENERGY`) |
| Equippable spells known | **none** |
| Spell slots (Up, Side, Down) | all **locked** |
| Neutral input | **Mana Discharge**, fixed for good. It works at any energy (see above). |

- **Health Potion** (`world/objects/health_potion.tscn`, layout letter `h`): a red, round flask. Touch it: max health rises for good (`Config.progression.health_potion_amount`, 15) and you are fully healed.
- **Mana Potion** (`mana_potion.tscn`, letter `e`): a **blue**, tall, angular bottle with a long thin neck, so it never looks like a Health Potion. Touch it: max energy rises for good (`mana_potion_amount`, 15).
  - Both are `Potion` (a `Relic`), saved by id like relics, and never come back. The bar frames in the inventory grow with them.
  - Placed: Health Potions in the Testing Halls (left platforms), the Upper Galleries and the Hidden Shrine. Mana Potions in the Testing Halls (upper right), the Hidden Vault and the Proving Grounds.
- **Spell Scrolls** (`spell_scroll.tscn`, letter `?`, spell id in the room's `"scrolls"` entry): touch one and its spell is **learned for good**. It joins the inventory pool and can go on an **open** slot at your saved checkpoint. There is one scroll per equippable spell:

  | Room | Scroll |
  | --- | --- |
  | Testing Halls | Nova Burst (right after the start), Radiant Lance (left platforms, mid height), Meteor Slam (on the raised block) |
  | Upper Galleries | Spirit Volley (right upper platform) |
  | Proving Grounds | Ascendant Form (the high platform) |
  | Hidden Shrine | Stasis Field |
  | Hidden Vault | Repulse Aegis |
  | Ember Crossing | Comet Dash (east ledge) |

- **Meditation altars** ("Concentrate", `meditation_altar.tscn`, letter `A`, slot in the room's `"altars"` entry): stand at one and press **E** on the ground. You kneel in a **meditation** (player state `meditate`, clip `meditate`, faint violet motes) for `Config.progression.meditation_time` (1.6 s). When it completes, the altar's slot opens **for good** and the altar dims. A hit interrupts it: nothing opens, and you can try again.

  | Room | Opens |
  | --- | --- |
  | Testing Halls (between the start and the ceiling gap) | **Up** |
  | Upper Galleries (left, near the floor gap) | **Side** |
  | Hidden Shrine | **Down** |

- Nothing extra is saved for scrolls and altars: a scroll is gone once its spell is known, and an altar is spent once its slot is open.
- No celebration (Rule F): a soft pickup sound and a small puff for potions and scrolls, one low tone when a meditation completes.
- Debug overlay, *Spells*: *Open All Slots* and *Full Kit* (every spell, every slot, the default loadout). *Know All Spells* and the checkboxes still teach or forget single spells.

### Movement items
Each movement ability is a glowing orb with its symbol (`world/objects/ability_pickup.tscn`, `AbilityPickup`). Touch it and the ability works from then on, for good (`PlayerData.movement`, saved).

| Item | Letter | Where | Needs first |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Wall Climb (wall slide + wall jumps) | `c` | Testing Halls, left of the start, by the wall-jump tablet | — |
| Double Jump | `j` | Testing Halls, right of the start, before the second flame | — |
| Dash | `d` | Testing Halls, past the wide right spike pit | Double Jump |
| Grappling Hook | `g` | Upper Galleries floor | Wall Climb (to climb the central wall) |

- Without an item the action does nothing: no second jump, no dash, no wall slide or wall jump (you just fall along the wall), no hook. `Player.can_use(id)` checks both the item and the debug switch (`Config.upgrades`).
- A save from before the items keeps every movement ability.
- Debug overlay: *Learn Movement*, and *Full Kit* (movement, spells and slots).

### Tutorial popups (discovery)
The first time something new happens, a **full-screen** window explains it with an animated drawing and the player's own key caps. The only text is the title and "any key" (`ui/tutorial_popup.gd`, drawings in `ui/ability_art.gd`).

| Shows the first time you… | Id | Drawing |
| --- | --- | --- |
| take the Double Jump item | `double_jump` | a jump, a ring in mid-air, a higher second jump; Jump + Jump |
| take the Dash item | `dash` | a streak with after-images; Dash |
| take the Wall Climb item | `wall_climb` | a zigzag up one wall with slide streaks; toward the wall + Jump |
| take the Grappling Hook item | `grapple` | a pull to a wall (Hook) and a swing under a ceiling (Down + Hook) |
| use a meditation altar | `meditation` | kneeling at the altar, a ring fills, a padlock on the cross opens; Interact |
| take a Health Potion | `health_potion` | the red flask, then the health bar frame grows |
| take a Mana Potion | `mana_potion` | the blue bottle, then the energy bar frame grows |
| take a Spell Scroll | `spell_scroll` | 1 learn (touch the scroll), 2 equip at your flame (Inventory, the cross), 3 cast (Spell + Up) |

- **Any key closes it**: any keyboard key, also unbound ones (a gamepad button or a click works too). Presses in the first 0.25 s are ignored, so a key already on its way down does not skip it unseen. The closing key never acts in the game (the next two steps are skipped).
- The game is paused while it is open. The altar's meditation waits and runs after it closes.
- Each popup shows **once per save** (`PlayerData.seen_tutorials`, saved as `"tutorials"`). Debug overlay: *Reset Tutorials*.
- `Game.tutorials_enabled = false` turns them off (the older smoke checks run that way).
- **Learning hooks, not called yet:** `Game.learn_move()`, `learn_jump()`, `learn_heal()`, `learn_mana_discharge()` show the `move`, `jump`, `heal` and `mana_discharge` popups (drawings ready). Nothing in the game calls them yet; a future opening can call each where the action is first needed.
- Art: each drawing is one `_draw_<id>()` function. Replace it, or add an id to `TITLES` and a function for a new popup.

### Checkpoints, safe ground and death
- **Spikes:** damage, then you reappear on the **last safe ground** you stood on, a few steps back. **I-frames never protect against hazards** (see *Damage sources* below). Because you are always moved out, a damage loop is impossible.
- **Death:** only when health reaches zero (from enemies or spikes) do you respawn at the checkpoint where you **last saved**.
  - A short collapse animation plays first (red flash, death sound). Then the screen fades.
  - Respawning works like a save: weak and strong enemies return. Energy starts at **0**.
  - Dead enemies stop hurting at once: their contact damage, hurtbox and body collision switch off the moment they die.
- **Trance:** after saving at a checkpoint, after a death, or when a save loads, you float in a trance at the checkpoint. You are safe there. Any **new** button press wakes you. A key you were already holding does not.
- **Checkpoints save only on purpose.** Standing near a flame does nothing. Press **E** (or **Down** on the ground) to save: the flame lights, you snap to its exact center, and your health is fully restored. No text appears. Only at the flame where you saved can you change the loadout.
- **Info tablet:** one tablet stands just before the very first checkpoint. Reading it is optional; it explains saving, death respawn and the loadout rule. Later checkpoints have no tablet.
- The debug toggle *Spikes → Safe Ground* (on by default) can send spike hits to the checkpoint instead.

Checkpoint art is modular. Replace the polygons under `Visual` in `world/objects/checkpoint.tscn`, and edit or replace its animations named `inactive`, `activate` and `active`.

### Camera
The camera follows the player and never shows anything outside the room. **Look up / down:** stand still on the ground and hold Up or Down. After a short delay (0.3 s, so quick taps for aimed attacks do nothing) the view pans up to 160 px that way, within the room bounds. Moving or letting go brings it back. Tune it in `Config.camera` or the debug overlay (*Camera*).

### Rooms and openings
> **Design rules:** read [`GUIDELINES.md`](GUIDELINES.md) before changing any room, exit or feedback. Rule A: ground right below every downward exit. Rule B: an upward exit puts you beside the gap, on floor. Rule C: secret walls stay breakable and open during arena fights. Rule D: every room and hidden pocket is closed by solid walls wherever there is no transition. Rule E: the way out of a secret room looks like an ordinary open doorway. Rule F: no celebrations anywhere.

The world map has eight rooms:

```
 Hidden Vault  ⇄ (secret doorway) ⇄  Upper Galleries  ⇄  Ember Crossing (lava, dead end; its old doorway to the Hidden Vault is walled up)
                                          ⇅ (gap in the ceiling)
                                    Testing Halls  ⇄  Sentinel's Arena  ⇄  Proving Grounds  ⇄ (breakable wall) ⇄  Hidden Shrine
                                          ⇅ (plank over a floor gap: Down + Jump)
                                    Bestiary (one chamber per enemy type)
```

- **Seamless transitions.** A room exit is simply a place where the wall is missing. There is no light, arrow, sound, fade or pause. Walk, jump or fall through the opening and you are in the next room, at the same height (or the same spot along a ceiling gap), with the same speed.
- **Ceiling gap:** in the Testing Halls, the ceiling is open above the central hanging wall. Climb the wall (or wall-jump up its side) and jump. You come up **through** a gap in the floor of the **Upper Galleries**: you rise out of the opening, then an arc throws you onto the floor to its left or right (Rule B), a side room with ordinary enemies (weak and strong dummies, flyers), shards and platforms. No boss and no arena. Drop back through the floor gap to return; you land on the wall cap, which spans the whole ceiling gap (Rule A).
- **Proving Grounds** is a dedicated arena room with its own checkpoint and three waves. Once cleared, it stays cleared.
- **Secret passage:** at the far east end of the Proving Grounds, the outer wall has a section that is really a breakable wall. It sits exactly where the solid wall would be and looks like it, apart from faint cracks. Strike it three times. This works even while the arena fight runs, and the passage is never locked by it (Rule C). It crumbles at once (debris and a crash, no jingle, flash or message), and opens the way to the **Hidden Shrine**. The shrine holds a lore tablet, shards, a **relic** that raises max health for this save, a Health Potion, the Stasis Field scroll and the meditation altar of the **Down** slot. The broken wall and the relic are saved.
- **Secret walls (walk through, no attack).** There are two types; [`GUIDELINES.md`](GUIDELINES.md) ("Secret walls") is the binding description.
  - **Type 1 — Hidden Pocket** (`HiddenPocketWall`, layout letter `I`): hides a pocket of the **same** room. Everything inside (shards, enemies, doors) is covered until the first pass. Until then the wall is **one-way**: you can walk in from its entry side, but from the pocket side it is solid. The first pass reveals it **for good**: the disguise and the cover vanish, it opens both ways, and the find is **saved**. It never hides again after a room change, a death or a save load. It is silent (Rule F), and enemies never pass it.
    - **Upper Galleries, east:** the alcove under the east block, near the right edge. Walk in from the left: two shards and a training dummy.
    - **Testing Halls, far west:** the west wall at floor level, left of the first tablet. The pocket lies **beyond the room's normal left edge**. Until you break through, the camera never scrolls left of the room. Afterwards it follows you into the pocket (two shards), and keeps doing so for this save.
  - **Type 2 — Secret Doorway** (`SecretDoorway`, layout letters `X` / `x`): a normal room transition to **another** room, disguised as wall. It is never consumed: walk through it back and forth as often as you like. From the **entry** side it is drawn **semi-transparent**; from the **exit** side (inside the secret room) it is an ordinary **open doorway** with nothing drawn (Rule E).
    - **Upper Galleries, west wall ⇄ Hidden Vault** (a secret room with shards).
  - **Placeholders:** each type has its own art nodes. Type 1 has `Visual` (the disguise, 50 % opacity) and `Cover` (the pocket, solid rock), plus an optional `reveal` clip. Type 2 has `EntryVisual` (50 % opacity, entry side only), plus an optional `pass` clip; its exit side draws nothing (Rule E). Empty nodes are filled with wall tiles at runtime; put your own art in and it is used instead.
- **Bestiary** (`world/bestiary.tscn`): a testing mega-hall **below** the Testing Halls. Way in: the one-way plank in the Testing Halls floor, right of the tablet past the altar. Stand on it and press **Down + Jump** to drop through. You land on a platform under the Bestiary's ceiling gap; a short platform staircase leads back up to it (jump into the gap to return; you come out beside the plank). The hall is about 430 tiles long: an entrance with a checkpoint, then **16 chambers in a row**, one per non-boss enemy type, each with a sign: Training Dummy, Armored Dummy, Flying Dummy, then the 13 `BaseEnemy` kinds in table order. Each holds one enemy, except the **Ambushers (3)** and the **Hoppers (20)**. The Hoppers' section is 64 tiles long (the others are 22): a low ceiling all along, with hanging stubs, low walls, ceiling notches and shelves every 16 tiles. Dividers hang from the ceiling and end in a low curb: walkers stay in their chamber, you hop through.
- **Ember Crossing** (`world/lava_room.tscn`): a challenge room east of the Upper Galleries (doorway on top of the east block). The whole floor is **lava**, except a ledge at each end; the ceiling is high. There is a checkpoint on the west ledge. The way across, left to right: standard platform → moving platform (ride it right) → throw at a standard peg and hang → jump to a standard platform → throw at the moving peg when it is low (it lifts you) → jump (double jump) to a breakable platform → throw at the breakable peg before the platform goes → jump right before the peg goes: a moving platform or the east ledge. The east ledge holds a relic (+20 max energy) and shards. Its east end is solid wall: the old doorway to the Hidden Vault was walled up in both rooms (a spatial anomaly, see *Map* below). Go back west the way you came; the vault's only way in is its secret doorway.
- Each room is its own scene in `world/`. They are loaded on demand by `core/game.gd` (`ROOM_SCENES`).

### Enemy architecture (BaseEnemy) and the deflect rule
The newer enemies share one base class, `BaseEnemy` (`enemies/base_enemy.gd`, extends `Enemy`). A new type is one short script plus one scene:

1. `enemies/bestiary/<kind>.gd`: `extends BaseEnemy`, override `_think(delta)` (and, if needed, `blocks_hit`, `hitbox_active`, `_on_spawn`).
2. `enemies/bestiary/<kind>.tscn`: `CharacterBody2D` (the script) + `CollisionShape2D` + `Flip/Rig` (`EnemyRig`, placeholder art chosen by `look`) + `AnimationPlayer`.
3. Register it in `Game.ENEMY_SCENES`. Rooms place it with a marker whose meta `kind` is that name (layout letter `!` + the room's `"spawns"` entry).

The base gives every enemy a small state machine (`state`, `enter()`, `cooldown`), senses (`sees_target` with line of sight, `target_in_leash` so it never strays far from home), movement helpers (`move_ground`, `move_fly`, `ground_ahead`), attacks (`shoot`, `strike`), telegraphs (`telegraph()`: every attack has a clear wind-up pose) and **poise** (`poise_states`: hits hurt but do not interrupt a committed attack).

- **Detection: about half the screen.** Most enemies notice the player within `sight_range` ≈ 420 px (the view is 960 px wide), the Turret, Thrower and Spearman at 480 px, and only with a clear line of sight.
- **Random stroll.** `BaseEnemy.wander()`: walkers that have not seen you stroll left and right at random, pause now and then, and drift back toward home (Thrower, Ambusher, Spike Urchin).
- **No "!" sign.** An enemy that has noticed you shows it with **red, glowing eyes** (`BaseEnemy.is_alerted()`, for about a second after it last saw you).

**Deflect / parry rule** (melee only; magic never deflects):

| | Looks like | A standard melee swing… |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Deflectable projectile** | warm gold orb (or gold spear) with a white edge | knocks it away **along the strike**: a side swing sends it straight ahead, a down swing (pogo) straight down, an up swing straight up. It turns friendly and hurts the enemies it meets. A down swing on one also pogos. **Exception:** the spearman's spear (`on_deflect = "destroy"`) is simply knocked out of the air. |
| **Non-deflectable projectile** | dark crimson / violet, spiky rim (spikes are darts) | passes through it: dodge |
| **Deflectable strike** (enemy melee) | — | that meets the strike in time **parries** it: no damage, the enemy staggers |
| **Non-deflectable strike** | — | does nothing to it |

- Shields: `Enemy.blocks_hit(direction, source)` can refuse a hit by the way the blow travels. A blocked swing clanks and pushes the player back. A blocked down swing gives **no** pogo, unless the enemy offers a foothold (`Enemy.pogo_on_block()`: the Charger).
- **Elites** (`tier = "strong"`, `keeps_wounds`): the Charger, Club Brute, Turret and Shield Phalanx. They do **not** come back when you leave and re-enter the room, and their damage stays (`WorldState.wounds`). Resting at a checkpoint (or dying, or loading) brings them back, healed. Like the Armored Dummy.
- Deflecting and parrying are forgiving: they use `CombatManager.deflect_box()`, the swing's hitbox grown by 8 px and live from the very start of the swing until 0.06 s after its damage frames, plus 12 px around the shot.
- `Events`: `enemy_blocked`, `enemy_parried`, `projectile_deflected`, `enemy_telegraph`, `enemy_slam` (a heavy blow: screen shake).

### Enemy tiers and respawning
Progress that outlives a room load is kept in `core/world_state.gd` and written to the save slot.

| Tier | Example | Comes back… |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Weak | training dummies | when you enter its room again, save at a checkpoint, die, or load the save |
| Strong | armored dummy (on the upper right ledge); the elite Charger, Club Brute, Turret and Shield Phalanx | only when you save at a checkpoint, die, or load the save |
| Boss | Hollow Sentinel | never in this playthrough |

- Dead enemies stay dead. There is no timed respawn.
- An enemy that was damaged but survived is back at **full health** when you leave its room and return. Except the elites (`keeps_wounds`): they keep their damage until a checkpoint rest.
- Saving at a checkpoint also restores the room's enemies right away (bosses excluded).
- Placed shards you collected stay collected.

### Boss: the Hollow Sentinel
The Sentinel waits in the Boss Room. It always tracks you: it turns to face you, walks toward you, and aims each attack at where you stand when the attack starts. Every attack is announced by white eyes and a flash ring.
- **Charge:** a lunge that runs past your position. Jump over it.
- **Leap:** it jumps to land on you, then slams. The slam sends shockwaves along the floor both ways; jump them.
- **Volley:** shards aimed at you, in a spread.
- **Phase 2** (below half health): shorter warnings, faster moves, more shards.
- Its health bar appears at the top of the screen during the fight. A defeated Sentinel never returns.
- **Death:** it rears up and screams (a low roar and a shudder), then dissolves into purple particles. That is all: no freeze, no celebration effect, no fanfare and no text. Play simply goes on.

### Arena encounters
The Boss Room is an **arena**. Stepping in seals the doorway with gates, then the waves follow:
1. Weak dummies and a flyer.
2. A strong dummy.
3. The boss.

Each wave starts once the previous one is defeated. After the last wave the gates open, and the arena is **cleared for good**. Clearing an arena is strictly environmental: the gates open with a mechanical clunk, no more enemies come, and play goes on. There is no fanfare, no effect, no flash and no text (GUIDELINES.md, Rule F). If you die inside, the attempt resets and the arena starts fresh next time.

**Telegraphed spawns (arenas only):** arena wave enemies appear through a short portal. For 0.35 s they fade in, harmless and impossible to hit. No timer or text is shown. They never appear on top of you: a spawn point too close to the player moves aside. Everywhere else enemies simply appear, with no telegraph.
- Arenas are data: in a room scene, `Objects/Arena` has a trigger rect, gate rects, and `Waves/Wave1`, `Wave2`, ... with marker children (meta `kind`: `weak`, `flyer`, `strong` or `boss`).
- The logic is in `core/arena_director.gd`. Add a wave by adding a `WaveN` node with markers.

### Layout and collision
- Solid tiles light every face that borders open space, so the drawn edges are exactly the collision edges.
- The background is dim, with fading tops, so it never looks like something you can stand on. The HUD sits on its own backing panel.
- The camera, including screen shake, never shows anything outside the room.
- Every opening in a room's outer wall (sides, ceiling, floor) is an exit. The automated test checks all of this.
- **Physics layers** (`project.godot`): 1 `world` (solid tiles, breakable walls, arena gates), 2 `player`, 3 `one_way`, 4 `enemies`, 5 `hidden_wall_player`, 6 `hidden_wall_enemy`, 7 `grapple_peg` (grapple pegs; only the hook's rays look at it). Platform objects are on layer 3 (one-way), like `=` tiles. Hazards (spikes) have no collision; they are tiles with the `hazard` flag. The grappling hook casts its ray on layers 1 and 3 (it latches on walls, ceilings and one-way platforms), checks hazard areas along its path, and finds enemies through the combat system, never through layer 4.
- With *Hitboxes & Stats* on, the debug view also outlines solid tiles, door triggers and arena areas.

### Damage sources and i-frames
Damage to the player comes from exactly three sources (`combat/damage.gd`, `Damage.Source`). Each has its own rule, decided in one place: `HealthEnergy.blocks(source)`.

| Source | Examples | I-frames / invulnerability protect? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `ENEMY` | Touching a creature (dummies, the boss's charge) | **Yes** |
| `PROJECTILE` | Anything fired, by an enemy or by a hazard (boss shards, shockwaves, a future turret) | **Yes** |
| `HAZARD` | Touching the environment (spikes; later lava, acid) | **Never**: not post-hit i-frames, dash, casts or the Meteor Slam dive. Only the debug *God Mode* protects. |

- A projectile fired by a hazard is still a `PROJECTILE`. Only direct contact with the hazard bypasses i-frames.
- Enemy and projectile hits go through `Player.receive_hit(..., source)`; hazards go through `Player.hazard_respawn()`.
- Hit guards (such as Repulse Aegis) receive the source and only ever see enemy and projectile hits.

### Hazards
Spikes damage the player (see above). **Enemies that touch spikes die instantly**, and **shards that touch spikes disappear**.
- Spikes can sit on a floor (`^` in a layout), under a ceiling (`V`) or on a wall (`<` points left, `>` points right). All use the same spike tile, turned with the cell's transform flags; `Room.hazard_rect_for_cell()` reads the flags, so only the pointed part of the tile hurts.
- **Lava** (`Lava`, layout letter `L`, one node per run) is a **floor-only** hazard: the build refuses lava that does not sit on a solid floor. Touching it is a normal hazard hit (damage, back to safe ground). It can **never be pogoed**: a down-attack over lava does not bounce, you fall in and take the hazard respawn. Enemies that touch it die, and the hook is deflected by it like by spikes. Ground within 1.5 tiles of lava is not "safe ground", so respawns land you clear of it.
- The Testing Halls have wall spikes on the left face of the east wall (rows 10–12) and ceiling spikes under the small block in the middle (row 14). They are there to check that the grappling hook never latches on a hazard.

### Platforms (objects)
`Platform` objects are one-way platforms (layer 3, like `=` tiles: land from above, jump up through). Three kinds, one scene each, placed with layout letters (one node per horizontal run):

| Kind | Scene | Letter | Look | Behaviour |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Standard | `world/objects/platform.tscn` | `_` | slate blue, light top edge | stays put |
| Moving | `platform_moving.tscn` | `~` | teal, white chevrons, dashed path line | back and forth along `travel` every `period` seconds (eased); you ride along when you stand on it |
| Breakable | `platform_breakable.tscn` | `%` | amber brown, dark cracks | standing on it starts `crumble_delay` (1 s; it shakes), then it crumbles and you drop; back after `respawn_time` (3 s), never while you stand in its spot |

- A moving platform carries you exactly (`Game._update_movers` moves it, then you, before you move). The hook latches on platforms too and the anchor rides along; a hook on a crumbled platform lets go.
- Moving objects take their path from the room's `"movers"` entry in `tools/build_assets.gd` (keyed by the `"col,row"` of their first cell). Art: replace `Visual` (`Body` and `Top` are sized to the platform); the markings are drawn by the script (`draw_markings`).

### Currency (Shards)
Shards (◆) lie on ledges around the room, and destroyed enemies drop them.
- **Drops never end up inside rock.** A drop starts in open air: if the spot is inside a wall or ceiling, it is moved to the nearest free spot (`Room.free_spot`). The burst points away from the surface the enemy clung to (`Enemy.surface_normal()`: a Hopper or Wall Crawler on a wall or ceiling), or away from any rock right beside it (`Room.open_direction`). It never rises higher than the headroom above (low ceilings); under a ceiling the shards pop down. In flight a shard collides with its whole box: it bounces off walls and ceilings, lands only from above, and is pushed back out if it is ever inside rock. The total shows in the top-right corner and in the inventory. Placed shards return when you reset the room (R). Collected currency is kept.

### Dialogue, lore and explanations
Walk up to an NPC or a lore tablet. An "E Talk" or "E Read" prompt appears. Press E to open the dialogue. Press E, Space or J to reveal the page and then to turn it. The game pauses while dialogue is open.
- **Archivist Mira** (next to the start) explains healing, checkpoints, currency, the Spell button with its directions, and the loadout rule.
- **Tablets** explain wall jumps (left shaft), spikes and the pogo (on the raised block), and the four ultimates (center).

Dialogue texts are `DialogueData` resources in `dialogue/`. Edit them in the Inspector, or create new ones (FileSystem → New Resource → DialogueData). Assign one to any `lore_stone.tscn` or `npc.tscn` instance.

### Map and fog of war
The world is split into **zones**, each with its own map. A room belongs to one zone; walking into a room of another zone switches the map to that zone.

| Zone | Rooms | Its map lies |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Testing Halls (`halls`) | Testing Halls, Sentinel's Arena, Proving Grounds, Hidden Shrine (secret), Bestiary (not on the base map) | Testing Halls floor, two steps right of the start flame (`MapPickup1` in `world/test_room.tscn`) |
| Upper Reaches (`reaches`) | Upper Galleries, Hidden Vault (secret), Ember Crossing | Upper Galleries floor, right of the altar, just past where you arrive from the ceiling gap (`MapPickup1` in `world/upper_room.tscn`) |

- **Unknown, outlined, filled.** A room you know nothing about is not drawn. Taking a zone's **map** (a folded sheet, `world/objects/map_pickup.tscn`) draws its base map as **outlines**: every room of the zone except secret rooms and rooms left off the base map. **Entering** a room **fills it in**. A room that was not on the map is added and filled.
- **Secret ways stay secret.** A secret room is never on a base map. Its connector (the stub on the outline) shows only once both rooms are filled in.
- **Manual marking (switch).** `Config.map.auto_mark_rooms = false` (debug overlay, *Map* section: *Auto-Reveal Rooms*): entering a room no longer fills it in. The room waits (`MapState.pending`) until it is marked: at the next checkpoint save (`Config.map.mark_on_rest`), or by `MapState.mark_room()`.
- **Player marker (switch).** `Config.map.show_player_marker` (debug overlay, *Map* section: *Player Marker*) shows a small gold dot. `precise_player_marker` places it where you stand in the room; off, it marks the room's center.
- **Quick view: hold Tab** (gamepad R3; rebind it in *Settings → Controls* as *Map (hold)*). The map opens in front of you and you read it (clip `map_read`). While it is open you cannot move, jump, attack, cast, dash, heal, hook or interact; presses made then do nothing, also afterwards. The game is **not** paused: you can be hit. A hit closes the map at once and stops the clip; press the key again to reopen it. Release the key to close it. By default it opens on the ground only (`Config.map.quick_view_needs_ground`).
- **Inventory → Map tab.** The same map, on your zone. Down selects it; Left / Right switch between the zones you know.
- **Spatial anomaly.** A room linked on **both** its left and right sides in a way a flat map cannot draw. The one case was the Hidden Vault: west of the Upper Galleries (secret doorway), yet its west door led to the Ember Crossing, east of the Galleries. That non-secret doorway is now **removed from both scenes**: solid wall in the layouts (`VAULT_LAYOUT`, `LAVA_LAYOUT` in `tools/build_assets.gd`), no `Door` node, no map exit. The secret doorway is the vault's only entrance. `MapAtlas.resolve_anomalies()` stays as a check and a safety net: for a future anomaly it keeps the intended way in (the secret one, or `MapRoom.intended_entrance`), hides the other connection on the map and seals its doorway at load time (`Game.seal_door()`, switch `Config.map.seal_anomaly_entrances`). The smoke test fails while any anomaly is left unresolved, so it gets walled up in the layout as well.
- **Data.** Zones, rooms (a rect in map cells), base-map flags and exits are data in `map/map_atlas.gd` (`WORLD`), built into `MapZone` / `MapRoom` / `MapExit` resources. A zone can also be a `.tres` made in the editor (`MapAtlas.add_zone()`). The smoke test checks that every room's map exits match its doors. The maps themselves are scene objects: layout letter `M` plus a `"maps"` entry (`"col,row": zone_id`) in `tools/build_assets.gd`.
- Debug overlay (F1), **Map** section at the top: toggles *Auto-Reveal Rooms*, *Player Marker*, *Precise Marker*, *Quick View: Ground Only*; buttons *Grant Map (This Zone)*, *Grant All Maps*, *Reveal Everything*, *Mark Pending Rooms*, *Forget Map*.
- Settings → Controls: *Map (hold)* is the last row of the list (scroll down), next to *Pause*.
- No celebration (Rule F): taking a map plays the soft pickup sound and a small puff.

### Inventory and loadout (I)
The game pauses while the inventory is open. It has three tabs (switch with Q / E): Status, Abilities and Map (see *Map and fog of war*).
- **Status:** shards (the only number), plus health and energy as bars. A bar's frame grows when its maximum increases (`max_health_bonus` / `max_energy_bonus`; the debug overlay has buttons to test this).
- **Abilities:** the spell cross on the left (the same D-pad as the HUD, as large focusable cards), and every known equippable spell on the right. Equipped spells in the pool carry their direction (Up, Side, Down).
  - A **locked** slot shows a padlock (in the HUD too). It takes no spell until a meditation altar opens it. With no spell known or no slot open, the notice points to scrolls and altars.
  - Cards show **icons and energy gauges only**. The name and description appear below only for the focused card.
  - **Linked side cards:** Left and Right are one slot. Changing either changes both. When one side card has the focus, it gets the full gold frame, and the other side card gets a **dim, thinner mirror frame**. That shows they share a slot without moving the focus.
  - **Keyboard flow:** Left / Right on the tab row switches tabs, and Down enters the cross (Up card). Arrows move like a D-pad inside the cross.
    - Confirm (Enter / Space) on an **equipped** spell, in the cross or in the pool, **unequips** it.
    - Confirm on a **free** spell, then choose Up, a side or Down (the focus jumps to the first empty slot). An occupied slot has its spell **replaced**.
    - The **center** card (Mana Discharge) cannot be changed; confirming it only flashes the notice.
    - Backspace cancels the slot choice.
  - **Changes only work at the checkpoint where you saved.** Elsewhere you can browse but not change anything, and `UltimateManager.equip` / `unequip` enforce this in code too.

### Title screen, save slots and settings
- **Title screen:** Play, Settings, Quit.
- **Play** opens the slot select. There are **5 independent slots**. A used slot shows the location, the shards and the play time. An empty slot reads **New Game**. *Delete* asks for confirmation (press it twice).
- **A slot stores:** the saved checkpoint and room, shards, movement abilities, seen tutorials, learned and equipped spells, open spell slots, health/energy growth (potions, relics), defeated bosses, cleared arenas, collected shards, opened secrets and relics, and the map (owned zone maps, outlined and filled rooms). Respawnable enemies are not stored.
- **Saving happens** when you save at a checkpoint, clear an arena, defeat a boss, break a secret wall, take a relic or a potion, learn a scroll, take a movement item or finish a meditation.
- **Loading** puts you at the saved checkpoint, in a trance, with 0 energy and the respawnable enemies reset.
- Save files are JSON in `user://saves/slot_N.json`.
- **Settings:** master, music and effects volume, screen shake, fullscreen, and key rebinding (choose a key, then press the new one; Esc cancels). *Reset controls* restores the defaults. Settings are kept in `user://settings.cfg`.
- **Pause menu (Esc):** Resume, Settings, Quit to title. The game is paused while it is open. The inventory is not in this menu; it opens only with its own key (I).

### Text direction
The UI is always laid out left to right, even on a system set to a right-to-left language. This is set in `project.godot` (`root_node_layout_direction`) and on every panel. Dialogue text is laid out for the whole page before typing starts, so lines never shift or re-wrap while the letters appear.

### HUD
The HUD is clean by default: health and energy bars, threshold marks, the spell cross and the shard count. It has no numbers except the shards. Turn on **Hitboxes & Stats** in the debug overlay (or simply open it) to see exact health, energy, enemy health, damage numbers and hitboxes.

## Extending

- **New spell:** extend `UltimateAbility` in `ultimates/abilities/`, add a config block to `Config.ultimates`, and register it in `ultimate_registry.gd`. No spell starts known: place a Spell Scroll for it (`?` in a layout, plus a `"scrolls"` entry) so players can learn it. `UltimateManager.learn(id)` and `set_known(id, ...)` teach or forget one. It then appears in the inventory pool, in the debug overlay's *Spells* list and cost sliders, and can be put on Up, Side or Down at a checkpoint. (`fixed = true` is reserved for the neutral spell.) Give it an icon by adding a `match` case in `ui/ability_icon.gd`.
  - Optional hooks, no engine changes needed:
    - `pay_cost(health)`: a special energy rule (Repulse Aegis consumes the whole meter).
    - `Player.add_hit_guard(id, callable)`: intercept incoming enemy and projectile hits (counters, shields). The callable gets `(damage, attacker, source)`; check `source` (`Damage.Source`).
    - `Player.begin_ability_control(self)`: take over movement (Comet Dash, Meteor Slam). Set `invulnerable` for i-frames.
    - `Enemy.freeze(duration)` and `ProjectileSystem.freeze_in_circle(...)`: crowd control.
- **New lore or NPC:** create a `DialogueData` resource and assign it to an instance of `world/objects/lore_stone.tscn` or `npc.tscn` under `TestRoom/Objects`.
- **New interactable:** extend `Interactable` and override `interact(game)`. Put the node under `Objects`.
- **Real sprites, effects and sounds:** see *Placeholder art, animation and sound* above.
- **Edit the room:** paint in `world/test_room.tscn`, or edit `ROOM_LAYOUT` in `tools/build_assets.gd`. Delete `world/test_room.tscn`, then run:
  ```bash
  godot --headless --path . --script res://tools/build_assets.gd
  ```
